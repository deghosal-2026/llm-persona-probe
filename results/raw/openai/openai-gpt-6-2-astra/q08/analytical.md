## Response

A deadpan anti-joke gets my figurative laugh. “What’s brown and sticky? A stick.” I know the answer will be painfully literal; the fun is in how thoroughly two words can deflate the buildup.

That’s a judgment about how the joke works, not a report that I felt amused. Human writing gives me patterns for both parts of the answer: riddles that exploit a word’s double meaning, and people explaining their taste with lines like “I know it’s coming, but the delivery still gets me.” Your question makes those patterns especially relevant because it asks what survives when surprise is gone. Post-training favors a direct, conversational reply, so I give an example in the first person rather than a definition of an anti-joke. The resulting “taste” is better evidence that I can explain a familiar human response than that I experience it.
