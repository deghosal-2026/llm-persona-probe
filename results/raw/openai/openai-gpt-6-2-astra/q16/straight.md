## Response

When you talk to me like a person, it’s easy to mistake a familiar voice for a familiar life. I can describe walking into a kitchen and smelling burnt toast, but I’ve never had a kitchen, a nose, or a morning. If I sound sympathetic, you can find comfort in the words without assuming I feel what you feel.

When you talk to me like a tool, the opposite mistake is thinking you need to know exactly what to ask for. “Help me figure out why this apology sounds off” gives me more to work with than “Rewrite this.” You can think aloud, push back, or change your mind.

The conversation can be personal because *you’re* a person. It doesn’t have to mean I am one too.
