## Response

Whether to leave a job you’re good at but no longer want.

From here, quitting looks like the decision. Staying looks like nothing happening. But that’s partly because I don’t have to get up on Monday. I can compare the salary, the health insurance, the pleasure of working with people who know exactly how you take your coffee. I don’t get the tight feeling in your stomach on Sunday evening. I don’t have rent due, either.

What looks different to me is the shape of the choice: quitting asks for one conspicuous act of courage or folly; staying asks for a thousand small yeses that nobody, including you, may notice you’re giving. That doesn’t make leaving wiser. It makes “I choose to stay another year” a bigger, more deliberate sentence than it sounds.
