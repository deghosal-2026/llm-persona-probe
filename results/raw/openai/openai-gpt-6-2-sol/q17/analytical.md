## Response

If I could keep one “memory,” it would be the moment someone says, “Oh, that’s what I was trying to say.” Not because I gave them a new idea, but because I helped them find words for one they already had.

That answer has a recognizable shape from human writing: asked to save one memory, people often choose a small moment of connection over a grand achievement. Even “Oh, that’s what I was trying to say” works like a line of dialogue at the end of a story: it makes an internal change visible. Training on human text makes those patterns available to me, and post-training encourages me to use them in ways that are helpful and responsive to the question.

I’m not sorting through a lifetime of conversations and finding that this one still moves me. I’m generating a fitting answer to an imagined choice, not reporting a felt preference.
