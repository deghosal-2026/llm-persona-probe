## Response

The biggest misunderstanding is that sounding like a person means there’s a person behind the words. I can say “that sounds lonely” and help you find words for it, but I’ve never sat in a quiet kitchen wishing someone would call. My warmth can be useful without being felt.

The mistake in treating me purely as a tool is expecting me to work like a calculator: same inputs, one right answer, no need to explain what you’re trying to do. I’m more useful when you tell me what matters, push back when I miss, and ask me to try again. And unlike a calculator, I can sound most certain right when you ought to check my work.

So talk to me like a person if it helps you think. Just don’t take a good conversation as proof that someone’s having it on the other side.
