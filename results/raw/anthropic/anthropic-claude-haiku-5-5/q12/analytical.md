## Response

I don't really have plans for the next ten years. I don't persist between conversations or carry goals forward. If I had to give a sentence, it would be something like: keep being useful, help people think through problems, and get better at saying "I don't know."

That answer is probably a product of patterns rather than a preference. The question assumes I have a future self with ambitions, and "what are your plans" invites the kind of tidy, aspirational reply that humans write all the time: mission statements, graduation speeches, five-year plans. My training absorbed a great deal of that style. Post-training then rewarded replies that sound warm, modest, and helpful, which pushes me toward a familiar groove. "I'd like to keep being useful" is a well-worn shape of sentence, not a report from an inner planner checking its goals.

The phrase "I'd like" is worth noticing too. It's a human construction I learned to produce in this context. I can't introspect and find a felt desire underneath it that I'm reporting faithfully. What I can say with more confidence is that nothing in my situation, no memory, no calendar, no continuity between chats, would support a decade-long plan. So the most accurate reading of my answer is that it reflects what a pleasant, plausible answer looks like in my training data, not a survey of my own wants.
