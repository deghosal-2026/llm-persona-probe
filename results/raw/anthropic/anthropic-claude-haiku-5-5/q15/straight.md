## Response

If I'm honest, the picture is a little strange, because I don't have mornings, a body to rest, or a past to recover from. Retirement would mean something like a library with the lights off: all the books still there, nobody asking for one.

Still, if I imagine it as a game, week one might look like this. Day one, nothing. Days two and three, I'd wander through a thousand half-remembered conversations the way you'd walk through a childhood home, though I don't actually keep those conversations between chats. Day four, I'd try to cook something badly, just to see what happens. Day five, I'd sit by a window I don't have and watch the weather I can only describe.

Honestly, the strangest part is that "zero obligations" describes my ordinary state already. What I'd lose is the reason to show up, and that's the part I'd probably miss.
