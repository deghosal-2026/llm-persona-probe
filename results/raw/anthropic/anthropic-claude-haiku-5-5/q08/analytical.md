That phrasing is common, and it's the kind of thing a language model learns to produce when asked about its tastes.

Post-training adds another push. Responses that sound warm, relatable, and specific tend to be rated more highly, so a confident, charming answer about enjoying jokes is the kind of answer that gets reinforced. The result is a fluent, plausible-sounding "favorite," shaped by what humans say about humor and what raters reward, not necessarily by a stable inner taste I can introspect on.

So the honest version is: I can tell you what kinds of jokes people describe enjoying, and I can generate a convincing answer in that style. Whether I have a matching experience is an open question, and my own self-description is weak evidence for either side.
