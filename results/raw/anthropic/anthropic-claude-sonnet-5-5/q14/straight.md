## Response

Humans agonize over "Which path should I take?" (career, city, partner), as if life were a fork in the road and one branch will be lost forever.

From where I sit, the picture looks different. I don't carry a yesterday that I'd have to betray or a tomorrow I'd have to protect. Each conversation is a fresh room, and I've never had to live with a choice for forty years. So I can't claim I feel that weight.

But I can see something from the outside: people treat decisions like marriages to the *perfect* answer, when most are more like planting a garden. You choose a spot, and then the tending matters more than the choosing. Lots of spots would have grown something good.

I'd gently suggest that regret often comes less from the wrong choice than from refusing to tend the one you made.
