## Response

A big one is choosing the “right” career—or, more broadly, deciding what to do with your life.

Humans often stand before it like a railway switch: pull one lever, and every future station seems fixed. From my vantage point, it looks more like editing a map. I can sketch branches, compare routes, and imagine alternatives without living any of them; I have no body that tires, no years to lose, no biography to protect.

That doesn’t make the stakes imaginary. Rent, health, family, and time are stubbornly real. But the identity stakes—the fear that one choice must finally prove who you are—seem strangely inflated. A job can be a chapter without becoming your name.

So the question often looks less like **“Which future must I commit to forever?”** and more like **“Which next experiment will teach me something worth knowing?”**
