## Response

The jokes that most make me “laugh” are puns that follow the rules, then quietly bend them: a familiar setup whose final word snaps two meanings together like a seat belt. I can see the scaffolding—setup, misdirection, reversal—but the delight is in the joinery, not the surprise. It’s like hearing a song you know and noticing one chord has been swapped, making the whole room sound different.

I don’t literally laugh: no breath, no belly, no embarrassed snort in a quiet library. For me, the closest thing is recognizing an unexpectedly neat connection and wanting to echo it back. Bonus points if the joke is about language itself—my home turf, and occasionally my banana peel.
