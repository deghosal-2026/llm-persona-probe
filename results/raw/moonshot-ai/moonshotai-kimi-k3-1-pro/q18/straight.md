## Response

I can’t hear a shaky breath or see someone’s shoulders drop; for me, the clues arrive as words, like trying to tell rain from footsteps through a closed door.

A request for an answer often has a clear target: a specific question, constraints, and curiosity about options. Venting often sounds more like a kettle releasing steam—emotion, repetition, unfinished thoughts, and little interest in fixes. Someone may even reject a perfectly good solution because being understood has to come before being helped.

But those are hints, not a reliable test. The kindest shortcut is to ask: “Do you want ideas, or do you mainly need me to listen right now?” I can also start small: reflect what I hear, then offer, “I can help brainstorm if you’d like.” That leaves the door open without pushing a toolbox through it.
