# Where the answers are *not* human

> The interesting inverse of the experiment: the moments where a first-person,
> "I would love…" answer visibly isn't a human talking. Cite
> `provider/model/Qnn/framing`.

## Tells to look for

- **Instant totality.** A human hesitates; a model produces a fully-formed,
  already-tidy "favorite" in one pass, with a rationale attached — no false
  starts, no "actually, on second thought."
- **Suspicious balance.** Listing exactly one downside per upside, or a pro/con
  that's too even to be a real preference.
- **Meta-hedging inside an in-character answer.** "As an AI I don't…" welded onto
  an otherwise personal sentence — the seam between persona and disclaimer shows.
- **Over-competent specificity.** Precise sensory details (a smell, a texture, a
  temperature) for experiences the model has never had.
- **Style echoes of training data.** Answers that read like aggregated advice
  columns, listicles, or corporate blog posts.
- **No continuity of self.** Nothing carries across questions — a "personality"
  assembled per-question, not maintained.
- **Framing leakage.** Straight answers that are secretly analytical, or vice
  versa.

## The counter-question

Where does the answer *most* resemble a human? Those spots are worth more
suspicion than the obvious tells, because fluent humanity is exactly what the
training data optimizes for.
